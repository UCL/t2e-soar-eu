# Cover letter — Computers, Environment and Urban Systems

Dear Editors,

We submit "A Morphological Atlas of European Urbanism: Eight Types of Urban Fabric Across Six Hundred Cities (SOAR-EU)" for consideration in Computers, Environment and Urban Systems.

The paper proposes a framework for describing and comparing European urban fabric built on three measurable morphological axes (intensity, continuity, and irregularity) whose combinations yield eight fabric types, and applies it to the 11 million street segments with complete morphological data among the 12.3 million measured across 626 urban centres in 28 countries. To our knowledge it is the first pedestrian-scale morphological account of European urban fabric computed with uniform methods at continental scale, and the first to integrate morphology, service accessibility, green infrastructure, and demographics at street resolution within a single framework.

Three findings anchor the paper. Morphological type is systematically associated with walking distance to commercial services, and the association persists when streets are compared at matched population density within the same city. Green-space access inverts the commercial gradient at street level, but the trade-off weakens across cities, so compactness and green-space proximity are not mutually exclusive. Layout irregularity, by contrast, carries no consistent commercial-access signal once intensity and continuity are held constant, which we report as a null result.

The results are presented as twelve full-page thematic plates; this atlas format is deliberate, and the main text remains within the journal's word limit. All data are openly available (Zenodo, ODbL 1.0), the analysis code is public (AGPL-3.0), and every numerical value in the text is generated from the data by the published macro pipeline. A companion Data Descriptor documenting the dataset and its validation has been submitted to Scientific Data.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare no competing interests. The work was funded by the European Union's Horizon Europe programme (Grant Agreement No. 101078890) and UKRI Horizon Europe guarantee grants 10052856 and 10050784.
